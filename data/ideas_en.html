What China taught me

China is reminding the West of things it once knew. These are my latest insights and arguments.

  1. 01

    The West needs a Renaissance. Again.

    China shows deep cultural pride while the West has lost confidence in its own inheritance. My Toronto education taught me Canada had no culture. The West should teach its achievements alongside its failures and retell its own story.

    The West Needs A Renaissance ↗Law as Liberty ↗

  2. 02

    China is resuming its place in the world

    China spent a century as the “Sick Man of Asia” and has been recovering since 1978. I see a country resuming its historic place in the world. A stable world needs the West to understand that and compete well.

    You Need to Visit China. Now. ↗

  3. 03

    The West is learning the wrong lesson from China

    Many people look at China’s rise and conclude the West needs bigger government. I arrived in Zhengzhou in 2004 and watched the opposite happen. Growth took off after 1978, when Deng’s reforms reduced state intervention, starting in Shenzhen. That is why you have never heard of a North Korean startup. The West has forgotten the power of its own idea.

    Issue 3: You’re going to look at China and see the wrong thing →Lessons From the Chinese Economic “Miracle” ↗Where Are the North Korean Startups? ↗

  4. 04

    Study China’s rise to protect the West

    The West sorts everything into for China or against China. That is the wrong question. The line I care about is freedom versus tyranny, and you can only tell which side something is on by studying it. Go and see Shenzhen and Hangzhou for yourself before you decide what you think.

  5. 05

    Shutting China out weakens the West

    Every Western company, school and newsroom that pulls out of China leaves its place to someone who stays. The loudest calls to cut China out have cost the West a position in Asia that took decades to build. You compete with a rival by staying in the room.

    Quotes on geopolitics →

  6. 06

    Shenzhen’s civil war is going global

    More than half of the 1,900 exhibitors at IFA 2026 in Berlin were Chinese. Companies hardened by fierce competition at home are now competing with each other in Europe and America. If you sell there, you’re up against them too.

    Shenzhen’s Civil War Has Moved to Berlin ↗Quotes on competition →

  7. 07

    Trust decides who lasts abroad

    A good product and a fair price open the door to a new market. Staying there takes trust. After working with 500+ companies, I see the same test for a Chinese company in Ohio and a Western company in China.

    Trust: The New Battleground ↗Get the book: Trust (English e-book) →Quotes on trust →

  8. 08

    Human connection is the edge AI can’t copy

    AI can take the busy work and make a company faster. It cannot build trust for you. People still fly across the world to meet over coffee, because business runs on human connection. Use AI inside your company, and keep your outreach to clients and partners human.

  9. 09

    The West can compete

    Market reforms, a hungry can-do attitude and quick responses are enough to compete with China. Much of Asia’s edge comes from answering quickly and following through, and subsidies explain less than people think. I clear immigration at Changi in under 15 seconds. Nothing stops the West from working like that.
